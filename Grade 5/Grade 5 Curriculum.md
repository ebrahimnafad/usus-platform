## 1. Tune Up

### 1. Multiply Fluently 1-Digit x 2-Digit Numbers
- **Standards:** 4.NBT.5
- **Prerequisites:** 4-9
- **Description:** Multiply 1 and 2-digit factors by applying the distributive property. Find and add partial products to get the total product. What's 6x29? 29 = 20+9, so 6x29 = (6x20)+(6x9) = 120+54 = 174. Start by using pictorial number bonds. Progress to using the partial products algorithm, and finally, just mental math.

### 2. Divide Fluently to 100
- **Standards:** 4.NBT.6
- **Prerequisites:** 4-14
- **Description:** Divide 2-digit dividends by 1-digit divisors by applying the distributive property. Find and add partial quotients to get the total quotient. What's 68÷4? 68 = 40+28, so 68÷4 = (40÷4)+(28÷4) = 10+7 = 17. Start by using pictorial number bonds. Progress to using the partial quotients algorithm, and finally, just mental math.

### 3. Factor Pairs
- **Standards:** 4.OA.4
- **Prerequisites:** 4-61
- **Description:** Use number bonds to check for divisibility and find factor pairs. Is 2 a factor of 35? No, so multiples of 2 including 4, 6, 8, and 10 are not factors. Is 3 a factor of 35? No, so multiples of 3 including 6 and 9 are not factors. Is 5 a factor of 35? Yes, since 35÷5 = 7 and 5x7 = 35. Factor pairs for 35 are 1 and 35, and 5 and 7.

### 4. Prime & Composite Numbers
- **Standards:** 4.OA.4
- **Prerequisites:** 4-62
- **Description:** Determine if numbers have 1 – or more than 1 – factor pair. Use number bonds to check for divisibility and factors. Is 23 prime or composite? It's not divisible by 2, so it's not divisible by 4, 6, 8, or 10. 23 is not divisible by 3, so it's not divisible by 6 or 9. It's not divisible by 5 or 7, so 1 and 23 is the only factor pair. 23 is prime.

### 5. Multiply to 10,000 with Area Models & Number Bonds: Part I
- **Standards:** 4.NBT.5
- **Prerequisites:** 4-9
- **Description:** Multiply multi-digit factors by applying the distributive property. Find and add partial products to get the total product. What's 4x863? In expanded form, 863 = 800+60+3, so 4x863 = (4x800)+(4x60)+(4x3) = 3,200 + 240 + 12 = 3,452. Start by multiplying concretely with area models. Transition to pictorial number bonds.

### 6. Multiply to 10,000 with Area Models & Number Bonds: Part II
- **Standards:** 4.NBT.5
- **Prerequisites:** 4-9
- **Description:** Use area models to multiply 2-digit factors. Find and add partial areas to get total areas. What's 46x35? Break both numbers into expanded form. Apply the distributive property. 46x35 = (40x30) + (40x5) + (6x30) + (6x5) = 1,200+200+180+30 = 1,610. Transition from concrete area models to pictorial number bonds.

### 7. Multiply to 10,000 with the Partial Products Algorithm
- **Standards:** 4.NBT.5
- **Prerequisites:** 4-36
- **Description:** Transition from number bonds to the more compact and abstract, partial products algorithm. What's 134 x 2? Since 134 = 100+30+4, apply the distributive property to get 2x134= (2x100) + (2x30) + (2x4) = 200+60+8 = 268. Multiply from left-to-right. Add partial products to get the total product.

### 8. Divide to 10,000 with Area Models & Number Bonds
- **Standards:** 4.NBT.6
- **Prerequisites:** 4-13
- **Description:** Divide using area models and number bonds. Break the total into parts that are easier to divide. Find and add partial quotients to get the total quotient. What's 201÷3? 201 = 180+21, so 201÷3 = (180÷3) +(21÷3) = 60+7 = 67. If 180÷3 is difficult, think in units and multiply. What's 18 tens ÷ 3? 3 x __ tens = 18 tens? 6 tens, or 60.

### 9. Divide to 10,000 with the Partial Quotients Algorithm
- **Standards:** 4.NBT.6
- **Prerequisites:** 4-48
- **Description:** Divide using the partial quotients algorithm. What's 276÷6? Think of 276 in parts – multiples that are easier to divide. First divide 240÷6 = 40. Write the partial quotient 40 on the right. Subtract and divide what's left. 276-240 = 36, and 36÷6 = 6. Write the partial quotient 6 on the right. Add the partial quotients to get the total quotient. 276÷6 = 46.

### 10. Divide to 10,000 with Remainders
- **Standards:** 4.NBT.6
- **Prerequisites:** 4-64
- **Description:** Use number bonds and the vertical, partial quotients algorithm to divide 3-digit numbers with remainders. Break the dividend into multiples that are easier to divide. Find and add partial quotients to get the total quotient. If a part is left that's less than the divisor, it's called the remainder.

### 11. Divide to 10,000 with Remainders in Geometric Patterns
- **Standards:** 4.OA.5
- **Prerequisites:** 4-67
- **Description:** Use patterns to reinforce the grouping model of division with remainders. If a group of 4 shapes (hexagon, circle, square, pentagon) repeats, what's the 50th shape? Since 50÷4= 12 R2, the group repeats 12 times and the 50th shape is a circle, the 2nd shape in the group of 4 shapes. 51st shape? 51÷3 = 12 R3, so it's a square.

### 12. Fraction Equivalence
- **Standards:** 4.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 4-84, 4-83
- **Description:** Rename fractions by reasoning about the number and size of their parts. 4/7 is how many fourteenths? Fourteenths are half the size of sevenths, so we need twice as many, or 8/14. 4/6 is how many thirds? Thirds are twice the size of sixths, so we need half as many, or 2/3.

### 13. Order Fractions
- **Standards:** 4.NF.2
- **Prerequisites:** 4-93
- **Description:** Use reasoning strategies to put fractions in order on a number line. Compare fractions indirectly by using benchmark numbers like 1/2 and 1, and by comparing the size of missing pieces. Recognize fractions that are greater because they have more, larger pieces, or less because they have fewer, smaller pieces.

### 14. Add & Subtract Fractions with Equal Denominators
- **Standards:** 4.NF.3
- **Prerequisites:** 4-101, 4-99
- **Description:** Generalize from adding whole numbers to adding fractions with equal denominators and sums > 1. What's 5/4 + 1/4? Make sense of fraction notation by writing units explicitly. 5 fourths + 1 fourth = 6 fourths, so 5/4 + 1/4 = 6/4 = 1 1/2. Develop skills to prepare for fractions and decimals with all 4 operations.

### 15. Multiply Whole Number x Fraction
- **Standards:** 4.NF.4
- **Prerequisites:** 4-98
- **Description:** Transition from adding to multiplying fractions. What's 2 x 2/5? Draw a bar model to see 2 groups of 2 fifths is 2 fifths + 2 fifths = 4 fifths. Using fraction notation, 2 x 2/5 = 2/5 + 2/5 = 4/5. Shortcut? 2 x 2/5 = (2x2)/5 = 4/5. What's 2 x 3/8? 2 x 3 eighths = (2x3) eighths = 6 eighths = 3 fourths, so 2 x 3/8 = (2x3)/8 = 6/8 = 3/4.

## 2. Multiply Whole Numbers

### 16. Multiply using the Standard Algorithm: Part I
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.5
- **Prerequisites:** 4-37
- **Description:** Use the standard algorithm to multiply single-digit and multi-digit numbers. Apply the distributive property and multiply quantities by quantities and units by units. "7 ones times 9 ones is 63 ones. Write a 3 in the ones place. Regroup 60 ones as 6 tens and write a 6 in the tens place." Focus on each digit's quantity and place value.

### 17. Multiply using the Standard Algorithm: Part II
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.5
- **Prerequisites:** 4-42
- **Description:** Use the standard algorithm to multiply multi-digit numbers. Multiply quantities by quantities and units by units. Regroup and record new quantities in the appropriate columns according to their new place values. "6 tens times 8 tens is 48 hundreds. Write an 8 in the hundreds place and a 4 in the thousands place."

### 18. Use the Multiplication Standard Algorithm in Equal Groups Word Problems
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.5
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 4-10
- **Description:** Use word problems to provide context for multiplication. Start with learning problems and transition to story problems that provide context. Given the number of equal groups and how many in each group, use a bar model to visualize their relationship to the total. Multiply using the standard algorithm.

### 19. Use the Multiplication Standard Algorithm in Comparison Word Problems
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.5
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 4-19 ○ 4-16
- **Description:** Use multiplication to compare quantities. Draw a bar model to visualize the multiplicative relationship between the smaller amount and the larger amount. Use the standard algorithm to scale (multiply) the smaller number by the scale factor – how many times the smaller number is the larger number – to find the larger number.

### 20. Perimeter Word Problems
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.5
- **Prerequisites:** 4-82 ○ 4-81
- **Description:** Use geometric measurement to provide context for multiplying. Find the perimeter of a regular polygon – a flat shape with equal sides and angles. Draw bar models to visualize the relationship between the length of each side and the total length of all sides. Multiply using the standard algorithm.

### 21. Composite Perimeter & Area of 2D Shapes
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.5
- **Prerequisites:** 3-104 ○ 3-103, 3-50
- **Description:** Find the composite perimeter and area of 2-dimensional shapes made from 2 or 3 adjacent rectangles. Start by solving problems with known side lengths. Transition to problems with unknown lengths. Use reasoning to solve for the unknown sides and perimeter. Add or subtract partial areas to find the total area.

### 22. Volume of Rectangular Prisms
- **Standards:** 5.MD.3, 5.MD.4
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 3-100
- **Description:** Generalize from length and area, to measuring volume – the number of unit cubes that fit inside a rectangular prism. Multiply length times width to get the area of the prism's base. Then scale the area of the base by the prism's height to get the prism's volume. Derive the formula for volume, V = Area of Base x H = (L x W) x H.

### 23. Composite Volume of Rectangular Prisms
- **Standards:** 5.MD.5
- **Prerequisites:** 5-22 ○ 3-103, 3-100
- **Description:** Find the composite (total) volume of a 3-dimensional solid made of 2 or 3 adjacent, rectangular prisms. Start by adding partial volumes that are side-by-side to get the total volume. Transition to problems where partial volumes can be added vertically, or subtracted from a rectangular prism to get the total volume.

### 24. Model Numbers in Expanded Form with Exponents
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.1, 5.NBT.2
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 4-73 ○ 4-12, 4-7
- **Description:** What's the expanded form for 23,514? It's 20,000+3,000+500+10+4. It's also 2x10,000 + 3x1,000 + 5x100 + 1x10 + 4x1. 10,000 is 10 to the 4th, 1,000 is 10 to the 3rd, 100 is 10 to the 2nd, 10 is 10 to the 1st, and 1 is 10 to the 0 power. Do the number of zeros follow a pattern? They match the exponent or power of 10.

## 3. Divide Whole Numbers

### 25. Divide by 2-Digit Divisors using an Area Model
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.6
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 5-24 ○ 4-48, 4-47
- **Description:** Use area models to find an unknown factor. What's 450÷18? Think of 450 as the area of a rectangle with side length 18. What's the other side? Break 450 into 360 and 90 – easier multiples of 18. 360÷18 = 20, 90÷18 = 5, and 20+5 = 25. Add partial side lengths to get the total side length – the unknown factor.

### 26. Divide by 2-Digit Divisors using Number Bonds
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.6
- **Prerequisites:** 5-25 ○ 5-25, 4-50, 4-48
- **Description:** Transition from concrete area models, to pictorial number bonds. What's 408÷17? Think in units of 10 and 100. 40 tens doesn't divide easily by 17, but 34 tens, or 340, does. 408 = 340+68, so 408÷17 = (340+68) ÷ 17 = (340÷17) + (68÷17) = 20+4 = 24. Find and add partial quotients to get the total quotient.

### 27. Divide by 2-Digit Divisors using the Partial Quotients Algorithm
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.6
- **Prerequisites:** 5-26 ○ 5-25, 4-50
- **Description:** Begin the transition to vertical algorithms. What's 384÷16? First divide 320, an easier multiple of 16. 320÷16 = 20, so write the partial quotient 20 on the right. Subtract and divide what's left. 384-320 = 64, and 64÷16 = 4. Write the partial quotient 4 on the right. Add the partial quotients to get the total quotient. 384÷16 = 24.

### 28. Volume Problems with 2-Digit Divisors
- **Standards:** 5.MD.3, 5.MD.4
- **Prerequisites:** 5-22 ○ 5-27
- **Description:** Use volume to provide context for division. If the volume of a rectangular prism is 455 and the area of the base is 35, what's the prism's height? Volume = Area of Base x Height, so work backward. Divide the volume by the area of the base. What's 455÷35? Divide using the partial quotients algorithm. The height is 13.

### 29. Order of Operations
- **Standards:** 5.OA.1
- **Prerequisites:** 4-55
- **Description:** Solve equations using the conventional order of operations. Add and subtract, and multiply and divide, from left to right. Multiply and divide before adding or subtracting. To make the order explicit and more clear, use grouping symbols. Solve expressions inside parentheses ( ) first, followed by brackets [ ], and braces { }.

### 30. Interpret Verbal Expressions
- **Standards:** 5.OA.1, 5.OA.2
- **Prerequisites:** 5-29
- **Description:** Given a verbal expression, write the corresponding arithmetic expression using numbers and symbols. Use grouping symbols to group numbers and operations explicitly. For practice, evaluate each expression using a written method or mental math. Transition from operations with single-digit to double-digit numbers.

### 31. Interpret Numeric Expressions
- **Standards:** 5.OA.1, 5.OA.2
- **Prerequisites:** 5-30
- **Description:** Given an arithmetic expression with numbers and symbols, write the corresponding, verbal expression. Use parentheses to group numbers and operations explicitly. Evaluate each expression using a written method or mental math. Transition from operations with single-digit numbers to double-digit numbers.

### 32. Sequential 2-Step Comparison Word Problems
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.6
- **Prerequisites:** 4-20 ○ 4-16, 3-17
- **Description:** Use word problems and bar models to compare 2 numbers both additively and multiplicatively. Given a number and how much greater or less it is than another number, find the other number and use the grouping model of division to find the scale factor. Start with learning problems and transition to story problems.

### 33. Simultaneous 2-Step Comparison Word Problems
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.6
- **Prerequisites:** 5-32
- **Description:** Solve comparison problems that require both additive and multiplicative thinking – at the same time. Given the difference between 2 unknown numbers and their scale factor, find the numbers or their sum. Use the sharing model of division, while laying the foundation for algebra and solving simultaneous equations.

### 34. Perimeter & Area Problems
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.6
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 4-56 ○ 4-54
- **Description:** Use geometric measurement to provide context for division, multiplication, and multistep word problems. If a rectangle's area is 714 and the short side is 21, what's its perimeter? Since the long side is 714÷21 = 34, the perimeter is the sum of all 4 sides, or the sum of the long and short sides, doubled. 2 x (21+34) = 110.

### 35. Compare Digits 1 Place Apart
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.1
- **Prerequisites:** 4-30, 4-29
- **Description:** Compare the values of digits in the hundreds and tens place, or the tens and ones place. What's the value of a 2 in the hundreds place compared to a 4 in the tens place? 200 is 160 more than 40 since 200-40 = 160. 200 is also 5 times 40 since 200÷40 = 5, and 5x40 = 200.

### 36. Compare Digits 2 Places Apart
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.1
- **Prerequisites:** 5-35 ○ 4-31, 4-46
- **Description:** Compare the values of digits in the thousands and tens place, or the hundreds and ones place. What's the value of a 2 in the hundreds place compared to a 4 in the ones place? 200 is 196 more than 4 since 200-4 = 196. 200 is also 50 times 4 since 200÷4 = 50, and 50x4 = 200.

## 4. Add & Subtract Fractions

### 37. Find Common Denominators: Part I
- **Standards:** 4.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 4-84, 4-83
- **Description:** Prepare for fraction operations by making common denominators. What's a common denominator for 1/4 and 1/8? When one denominator is a multiple of another, it's also a common denominator. 8 is a multiple of 4, so 8 is a common denominator. Add and subtract 1/4 and 1/8 by using the equivalent fractions 2/8 and 1/8.

### 38. Find Common Denominators: Part II
- **Standards:** 4.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 5-37 ○ 4-59
- **Description:** Find a common denominator when one denominator is not a multiple of the other. What's a common denominator for 1/2 and 1/3? Multiply the denominators to get 2x3 =6. When adding or subtracting 1/2 and 1/3, use the equivalent fractions 3/6 and 2/6 to make the units the same.

### 39. Add Fractions with Unlike Denominators: Part I
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 5-38 ○ 4-98
- **Description:** Add 2 fractions that don't have the same denominators, but one denominator is a multiple of the other. What's 1/3 + 1/9? 9 is a multiple of 3, so 9 is a common denominator and ninths are a common unit. Since 1/3 = 3/9, add 3/9 + 1/9 = 4/9. To add and subtract fractions, their units – the denominators – must be the same.

### 40. Add Fractions with Unlike Denominators: Part II
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 5-39
- **Description:** Add 2 fractions that don't have the same denominators, and one denominator is not a multiple of the other. What's 1/2 + 1/3? 6 is a multiple of both 2 and 3, so 6 is a common denominator and sixths are a common unit. Since 1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6, add 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6. Find a common unit by multiplying the denominators.

### 41. Add Mixed Numbers with Unlike Denominators
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 5-40 ○ 4-103, 4-102
- **Description:** Add mixed numbers with unlike denominators. What's 3 1/4 + 1 1/2? Break both numbers into parts. Find and add partial sums to get the total sum. Since 3+1 = 4 and 1/4 + 1/2 = 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4, the sum is the mixed number 4 3/4. Start with problems that don't require regrouping. Progress to problems that do.

### 42. Add Decimal Fractions with Tenths & Hundredths
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 4-114
- **Description:** Add tenths and hundredths concretely using Base 10 blocks. What's a common unit? Hundredths. If a flat is 1, each rod or column is 1/10 = 10/100. What's 1/10 + 24/100? 1/10 is 1 rod or 10/100. 10/100 + 24/100 = 34/100. Use Base 10 blocks to visualize equivalent fractions and add hundredths.

### 43. Add Decimal Fractions with Tenths, Hundredths & Thousandths
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 5-42
- **Description:** Provide enrichment by adding tenths, hundreths, and thousandths. What's 2/10 + 142/1,000? Since 2/10 = 200/1,000, the sum is 200/1,000 + 142/1,000 = 342/1,000. Add thousandths to lay the groundwork for understanding decimals to the thousandths place.

### 44. Subtract Fractions with Unlike Denominators: Part I
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 5-39 ○ 4-100, 4-84, 4-83
- **Description:** Subtract 2 fractions that don't have the same denominators, but one denominator is a multiple of the other. What's 1/2 - 1/4? 4 is a multiple of 2, so 4 is a common denominator and fourths are a common unit. Since 1/2 = 2/4, subtract 2/4 - 1/4 = 1/4. To subtract fractions, their units – the denominators – must be the same.

### 45. Subtract Fractions with Unlike Denominators: Part II
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 5-44
- **Description:** Subtract 2 fractions that don't have the same denominators, and one denominator is not a multiple of the other. What's 1/2 - 1/3? 6 is a multiple of 2 and 3, so 6 is a common denominator and sixths are a common unit. Since 1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6, subtract 3/6 - 2/6 = 1/6. Find a common unit by multiplying denominators.

### 46. Subtract Mixed Numbers with Unlike Denominators
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1
- **Prerequisites:** 5-45 ○ 4-103, 4-102
- **Description:** Subtract mixed numbers with unlike denominators. What's 4 3/4 - 1 1/2? Break both numbers into parts. Find and add partial differences to get the total difference. Since 4-1 = 3 and 3/4 - 1/2 = 3/4 - 2/4 = 1/4, the total difference is 3 + 1/4 = 3 1/4. Transition from problems that do not require regrouping, to problems that do.

### 47. Add and Subtract Fractions & Mixed Numbers with Like Denominators in Line Plots
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1, 5.MD.2
- **Prerequisites:** 5-46 ○ 4-106, 4-103
- **Description:** Use line plots to provide context for adding and subtracting fractions and mixed numbers. Plot frequency data measured to the nearest half, quarter, and eighth inch. Find the minimum and maximum values, mode (value observed most), and range (difference between the smallest and largest values).

### 48. Add and Subtract Fractions & Mixed Numbers with Unlike Denominators in Line Plots
- **Standards:** 5.NF.1, 5.MD.2
- **Prerequisites:** 5-47
- **Description:** Plot the frequency of data measured to the nearest half, quarter, and eighth inch. Find the minimum and maximum values, mode (value observed most), and range (difference between smallest and largest values). Find common denominators when units are not the same. Simplify and convert fractions to mixed numbers.

### 49. Add Fractions with Unlike Denominators Word Problems
- **Standards:** 5.NF.2
- **Prerequisites:** 5-40 ○ 4-104
- **Description:** Use bar models to add fractions with unequal denominators. Transition from easier denominators like fourths and eighths where one is the common denominator (eighths), to harder denominators like thirds and fourths that require a third, common denominator (twelfths). Sums can be proper (<1) or improper (≥1) fractions.

### 50. Subtract Fractions with Unlike Denominators Word Problems
- **Standards:** 5.NF.2
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 5-49
- **Description:** Use bar models to subtract fractions with unequal denominators. Transition from easier denominators like thirds and sixths where one is the common denominator (sixths), to harder denominators like fourths and fifths that require a third, common denominator (twentieths). Fractions can be proper (<1) or improper (≥1).

## 5. Multiply Fractions

### 51. Fraction x Whole Number - Breaking into 1s
- **Standards:** 5.NF.4
- **Prerequisites:** 4-99
- **Description:** Find a fraction of a whole number by breaking numbers into 1s. What's 1/3 x 2? Think of 2 as 1+1. Draw a bar model or number bond to see 1/3 x (1+1) = (1/3 x 1)+(1/3 x 1) = 1/3+1/3 = 2/3, or 2 x 1/3. Since 1/3 x 2 = 2 x 1/3, transition to using the commutative property, when ready. What's 4/5 x 2? It's 2 x 4/5 = 8/5 = 1 3/5.

### 52. Fraction x Whole Number - Multiples Case
- **Standards:** 5.NF.4
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 5-51 ○ 4-59
- **Description:** Find a fraction of a whole number in a special case – when the whole number is a multiple of the denominator. What's 1/3 x 21? 21 is a multiple of 3, so divide 21 into 3 equal parts using the sharing model of division. 1/3 x 21 = 21 ÷3 = 7. What's 2/3 x 21? Think proportionally. Since 2/3 = 2 x 1/3, it's 2 x (1/3 x 21) = 2 x 7 = 14.

### 53. Fraction x Whole Number - Non-Multiples Case*
- **Standards:** 5.NF.4
- **Prerequisites:** 5-52
- **Description:** Provide enrichment by multiplying whole numbers that are not multiples of the denominator. Instead of breaking numbers into all 1s, break them into a multiple of the denominator and 1s that are left. What's 1/3 x 7? 6 is a multiple of 3, so break 7 into 6+1. Multiply 1/3 x 7 = 1/3 x (6+1) = (1/3 x 6)+(1/3 x 1) = 2 + 1/3 = 2 1/3.

### 54. Unit Fraction x Unit Fraction
- **Standards:** 5.NF.4
- **Prerequisites:** 5-53 ○ 4-107
- **Description:** Multiply unit fractions to find a new unit fraction. What's 1/3 x 1/4? Draw 1/4 using a bar model on a number line. Divide 1/4 into 3 equal parts. What's 1 part? There are 12 parts in 1, so 1/3 x 1/4 = 1/12. Shortcut? Multiply the denominators. What's 1/2 x 1/5? 2x5 = 10, so the new denominator is 10, and the new unit fraction is 1/10.

### 55. Unit Fraction x Proper Fraction
- **Standards:** 5.NF.4
- **Prerequisites:** 5-54
- **Description:** Multiply unit fractions and proper fractions by breaking proper fractions into unit fractions. What's 1/4 x 2/3? 2/3 = 1/3 + 1/3, so multiply 1/4 x (1/3 + 1/3) = 1/12 + 1/12 = 2/12 and 2/12 = 1/6. Algorithm? Multiply numerators to get the number of each part. Multiply denominators to get the size of each part. What's 3/4 x 1/2? (3x1)/(4x2) = 3/8.

### 56. Proper Fraction x Proper Fraction
- **Standards:** 5.NF.4
- **Prerequisites:** 5-55
- **Description:** Multiply proper fractions by breaking one or both proper fractions into unit fractions. What's 4/5 x 2/3? Since 2/3 = 1/3 + 1/3, multiply 4/5 x (1/3 + 1/3) = 4/15 + 4/15 = 8/15. Algorithm? Multiply numerators to get the number of new parts. Multiply denominators to get the size of each part. What's 2/3 x 2/3? (2x2)/(3x3) = 4/9.

### 57. Proper Fraction x Improper Fraction
- **Standards:** 5.NF.4
- **Prerequisites:** 5-56
- **Description:** Use the algorithm to multiply fractions when one of the factors is an improper fraction. Multiply the numerators to get the number of new parts, then multiply the denominators to get the size of the new parts. What's 2/3 x 4/3? (2x4)/(3x3) = 8/9.

### 58. Special Multiples Case*

## 6. Divide Fractions

### 67. Whole Number ÷ Whole Number - Breaking into 1s
- **Standards:** 5.NF.3
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 5-51
- **Description:** Divide whole numbers that aren't multiples of the divisor. Break the total into ones and apply the distributive property. What's 2÷3? - **Prerequisites:** 5-111
- **Description:** Divide decimals by decimals by first multiplying the dividend and divisor by 10 or 100. What's 56.4 ÷ .2? Multiply both numbers by 10 and use the partial quotients algorithm to solve 564 ÷ 2. Since 564 ÷ 2 = 282, 56.4 ÷ .2 = 282. What's 6.3 ÷ .35? Solve 630 ÷ 35 instead. Since 630 ÷ 35 = 18, 6.3 ÷ .35 = 18.

### 113. Compare Digits in Decimal Numbers Multiplicatively: Part I
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.1, 5.NBT.7
- **Prerequisites:** 5-110 ○ 5-103
- **Description:** Compare a digit's value and the value of the same digit 1 or 2 places to the right or left. How much less is .2 than 2? .2 is 1.8 less than 2 since 2 - .2 = 1.8 and .2 + 1.8 = 2. What fraction of 2 is .2? .2 is 1 tenth of 2 since .2 ÷ 2 = .1 and .1 x 2 = .2. Compare additively to find the difference, and multiplicatively to find the scale factor.

### 114. Compare Digits in Decimal Numbers Multiplicatively: Part II
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.1, 5.NBT.7
- **Prerequisites:** 5-113
- **Description:** Compare a digit's value and the value of a different digit 1 place to the right. 5 is how much greater than .2? 5 is 4.8 greater than .2 since 5 - .2 = 4.8 and .2 + 4.8 = 5. 5 is how many times .2? 5 is 25 times .2 since 5 ÷ .2 = 25 and 25 x .2 = 5. Compare additively to find the difference, and multiplicatively to find the scale factor.

### 115. Compare Digits in Decimal Numbers Multiplicatively: Part III
- **Standards:** 5.NBT.1, 5.NBT.7
- **Prerequisites:** 5-114
- **Description:** Compare a digit's value and the value of a different digit 1 place to the left. .4 is how much less than 8? .4 is 7.6 less since 8 - .4 = 7.6. .4 is what fraction of 8? .4 is 1/20 of 8 since 8 ÷ .4 = 20 and 1/20 x 8 = 8/20 = 4/10 = .4. Compare additively to find the difference, and multiplicatively to find the scale factor.

### 118. Derive Unit Rates with Standard Units
- **Standards:** 5.MD.1
- **Prerequisites:** 4-52 ○ 5-27, 4-16
- **Description:** Derive unit rates – the number of smaller units in 1 larger unit – for units of length, weight, and liquid volume. If 189 feet = 63 yards, how many feet is 1 yard? Draw a bar model to visualize the relationship between feet and yards. 189 ÷ 63 = 3, so 3 feet = 1 yard. Thinking proportionally, 189 ft = 63 yd, 21 ft = 7 yd, 3 ft = 1 yd.

### 119. Derive Unit Rates with Metric Units
- **Standards:** 5.MD.1
- **Prerequisites:** 5-118 ○ 4-46
- **Description:** Derive unit rates for length, mass (weight), and liquid volume. Use prefixes (kilo=thousand, milli=thousandth, centi=hundredth) to make unit rates easier to remember. If 63,000 m = 63 km, how many meters is 1 kilometer? Use a bar model to see 1,000 m = 1 km. 63,000 m = 63 km, so 7,000 m = 7 km and 1,000 m = 1 km.

### 120. Derive Unit Rates with Clock & Calendar Time
- **Standards:** 5.MD.1

### 121. Larger to Smaller Standard Units
- **Standards:** 5.MD.1

- **Description:** Convert larger to smaller units of measure by multiplying and thinking proportionally. Provide unit rates if needed. 3 miles is how many feet? ### 123. Smaller to Larger Standard Units
- **Standards:** 5.MD.1

## 12. Geometry

### 129. Introduction to Coordinate Geometry
- **Standards:** 5.G.1, 5.G.2
- **Description:** Introduce coordinate geometry. Given a point in Quadrant I, find its coordinate pair. Given a coordinate pair, plot the point. The first number in a coordinate pair gives the number of units moving from (0,0) to the right (east) along the x-axis. The second number gives the number of units moving up (north) along the y-axis.

### 130. Relative & Cardinal Directions
- **Standards:** 5.G.1, 5.G.2
- **Prerequisites:** 5-129
- **Description:** Apply understanding of coordinate pairs by moving on a coordinate plane. Given the starting point and 2 subsequent points, determine the distance and relative direction to move (right, left, up, down). Given the starting point and the distance and cardinal direction to move (east, west, north, south), find the ending point.

### 131. Analyze Proportional Linear Relationships using Coordinate Pairs
- **Standards:** 5.OA.3, 5.G.2
- **Prerequisites:** 5-130
- **Description:** Analyze patterns and relationships using coordinate pairs. Starting at (0,0), use the rules for the x and y coordinates to make a table and plot the next 4 coordinate pairs. For every point, is the y coordinate the same multiple or fraction of the x coordinate? Since it's the same, the relationship between x and y is proportional.

### 132. Analyze Non-Proportional Linear Relationships using Coordinate Pairs*
- **Standards:** 5.OA.3, 5.G.2
- **Prerequisites:** 5-131
- **Description:** Enrich by analyzing coordinate pairs that do not start at (0,0). Start at a point on the y-axis where y > 0. Use the rules for the x and y coordinates to plot the next 4 coordinate pairs. Is the y coordinate always the same multiple or fraction of the x coordinate? Since it is not, the relationship between x and y is not proportional.

### 133. Classify & Compare 2D Shapes
- **Standards:** 5.G.3, 5.G.4
- **Prerequisites:** 4-134 ○ 4-133
- **Description:** Classify triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, and hexagons based on their defining attributes. Identify and compare the number of sides, equal sides, pairs of parallel sides, and right angles. Lay the foundation for understanding categories and subcategories by identifying and comparing shared attributes.

### 134. Explore Categories & Subcategories of 2D Shapes
- **Standards:** 5.G.3, 5.G.4
- **Prerequisites:** 5-133
- **Description:** Lay the foundation for understanding hierarchy by exploring categories and subcategories. With a square and a rectangle, which is the category and which is the subcategory? A square has 4 right angles and 4 equal sides, so it is a special rectangle. Rectangle is the category, and square is the subcategory.

### 135. Hierarchy of Quadrilaterals
- **Standards:** 5.G.3, 5.G.4
- **Prerequisites:** 5-134
- **Description:** Develop reasoning skills and fluency with 4-sided shapes and their defining attributes. Is a square always a rhombus? A square is always a rhombus because it is a parallelogram with equal sides. Is a rhombus always a square? A rhombus is not always a square because it does not always have 4 right angles.

## Overview

Fifth grade focuses on three critical areas: developing fluency with addition and subtraction of fractions and understanding multiplication and division of fractions (in limited cases); extending division to two-digit divisors while integrating decimal fractions into the place-value system and performing operations with decimals to hundredths; and developing understanding of volume. Students write and interpret numerical expressions, analyze patterns and relationships, and perform all four operations with multi-digit whole numbers and decimals using place-value understanding and properties of operations. Fraction operations expand: students add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators, multiply fractions, and divide unit fractions by whole numbers and whole numbers by unit fractions, applying these to word problems. Decimals are fully integrated as they read, write, compare, round, and compute with decimals to hundredths. Measurement & data involve converting measurements within a system and solving real-world problems, while geometry introduces the coordinate plane and classifying two-dimensional figures. Volume is explored by counting unit cubes, relating it to multiplication, and using formulas for rectangular prisms. These big ideas prepare students for the more abstract mathematics of middle school by emphasizing reasoning, modeling, and problem-solving across interconnected domains.
