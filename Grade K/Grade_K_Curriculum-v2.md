#### 1. Tune Up

**Question types in this unit:** Matching pairs by drawing lines (objects, shapes, solids, coins); coloring named shapes/solids; circling what comes next/first in patterns; "what is different / what does not belong" (X it).
**Tang lens:** Focuses on visual subitizing, sorting, and pattern recognition to establish 1-to-1 correspondence and perceptual grouping without counting. This unit prepares students for structural number representations in Unit 2 by anchoring comparison and attributes before digit modeling.

##### 1. Match Objects

- **Standards:** K.G.4
- **Description:** Comparing is a key aspect of learning. Given 2 columns of pictures, match each picture on the left with the same picture on the right. Explore what makes 2 pictures the same and what makes other pictures different. Matching activities introduce the concept of 1-to-1 correspondence. They help prepare students for counting.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Draw lines to connect each matching pair
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw lines to show which two are the same.
  - **Brain Builder:** WHAT IS DIFFERENT? - A: In each row, draw an X on the object that is different.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Concrete–pictorial–abstract — Compares matching pictures to build 1-to-1 correspondence and identify differences using visual logic puzzles.

##### 2. Match Shapes

- **Standards:** K.G.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-1
- **Description:** Introduce 2D shapes. Which shape on the left matches the same shape on the right? Matching sides and corners helps with one-to-one correspondence. Does a shape have an extra side or corner? Does it have more? If one shape has more, does the other shape have fewer? Match shapes to objects in the room.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Draw lines to connect each matching pair
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw lines to show which two are the same.
  - **Brain Builder:** WHAT IS DIFFERENT? - B: In each row, draw an X on the shape that is different.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Concrete–pictorial–abstract — Compares 2D shapes by matching sides and corners to explore one-to-one correspondence and visual attributes.

##### 3. Match Solids

- **Standards:** K.G.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-2
- **Description:** Introduce 3D solids. When possible, use real solids as well as pictures of solids. Recognize and learn their geometric names by making connections to common and familiar objects. Which solid looks like a can of soup? Which solid looks like an ice cream cone? Which looks like an ice cube? A box? A ball?
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Draw lines to connect each matching pair
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw lines to show which two are the same.
  - **Brain Builder:** WHAT IS DIFFERENT? - C: In each row, draw an X on the solid that is different.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Concrete–pictorial–abstract — Connects 3D geometric solids to common real-world objects using comparative reasoning and visual matching.

##### 4. Match Coins

- **Standards:** K.G.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-1
- **Description:** Introduce coins using real or plastic coins, and pictures of coins. Focus on color and size. Which coins are silver in color? Which coin is not silver? Which coin is the largest? Which coin is the smallest? Which is larger, a nickel or a penny? Which is smaller, a penny or a dime? Introduce and use comparison words.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Draw lines to connect each matching pair
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw lines to show which two are the same.
  - **Brain Builder:** WHAT IS DIFFERENT? - D: In each row, draw an X on the coin that is different.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Concrete–pictorial–abstract — Compares coins by color, size, and value terminology to build initial visual comparison skills.

##### 5. Sort and Group 2D Shapes

- **Standards:** K.MD.3, K.G.2
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-2
- **Description:** Identify and sort 2D shapes by coloring the same shapes the same color. Introduce part-total thinking. All the shapes are the total or whole. Each group of same-colored shapes is one of the parts. Together, the parts make the total. Is the total greater than each part? Which part has more shapes? Which has fewer shapes?
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** 1. Color the hexagons blue and the squares red. 2. Color the circles blue and the triangles red | 1. Color the squares blue and the circles red. 2. Color the hexagons blue and the triangles red | 1. Color the squares blue, the triangles red, and the circles green. 2. Color the circles blue, the hexagons red, and the triangles green
  - **Problem of the Day:** Cross out the flat shape that is different.
  - **Brain Builder:** SORT & GROUP - A: In each group, draw an X on the shape that does not belong.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Puzzle-based fluency — Uses shape sorting by color to introduce part-total visual classification and non-belonging puzzle logic.

##### 6. Sort and Group 3D Solids

- **Standards:** K.MD.3, K.G.2
- **Prerequisites:** K-3
- **Description:** Identify and sort 3D solids by coloring the same solids the same color. Encourage part-total thinking. All the solids are the total or whole. Each group of same-colored solids is one of the parts. Together, the parts make the whole. Is the total greater than each part? Which part has more solids? Which has fewer solids?
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** 1. Color the cubes blue and the cones red. 2. Color the spheres blue and the cylinders red | 1. Color the cones blue and the cylinders red. 2. Color the cubes blue and the spheres red | 1. Color the cylinders blue, the spheres red, and the cones green. 2. Color the cubes blue, the cones red, and the cylinders green
  - **Problem of the Day:** Cross out the solid shape that is different.
  - **Brain Builder:** SORT & GROUP - B: In each group, draw an X on the solid that does not belong.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Puzzle-based fluency — Classifies 3D solids by color to develop part-total grouping and identify solids that do not belong.

##### 7. Sort Shapes & Solids that vary in Size

- **Standards:** K.MD.3, K.G.2
- **Prerequisites:** K-6, K-5
- **Description:** Sort shapes and solids that vary in size. Is a large triangle still a triangle? Yes. Is a small hexagon still a hexagon? Yes. Changing the size of a shape or solid does not change what kind of shape or solid it is. Size describes – but does not define – a shape or solid. Size is a non-defining attribute, like color and orientation.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** 1. Color the triangles blue and the hexagons red. 2. Color the squares blue and the circles red | 1. Color the hexagons blue, the triangles red, and the circles green. 2. Color the triangles blue, the hexagons red, and the squares green | 1. Color the cylinders blue, the spheres red, and the cones green. 2. Color the cylinders blue, the cubes red, and the spheres green
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw small and large flat shapes. Trace the dotted lines. Color the small shape RED and the large shape BLUE.
  - **Brain Builder:** SORT & GROUP - C: In each group, draw an X on the shape that does not belong.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Puzzle-based fluency — Sorts shapes and solids across varying sizes to demonstrate that size is a non-defining visual attribute.

##### 8. Sort Shapes & Solids that vary in Orientation

- **Standards:** K.MD.3, K.G.2
- **Prerequisites:** K-7
- **Description:** When a shape or solid is rotated, does it change what kind of shape or solid it is? No. Is a triangle that's been rotated still a triangle? Yes. Is a cone that's been turned upside down still a cone? Yes. Orientation describes – but does not define – a shape or solid. Orientation is a non-defining attribute, like size and color.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** 1. Color the triangles blue and the hexagons red. 2. Color the circles blue and the squares red | 1. Color the circles blue, the triangles red, and the hexagons green. 2. Color the triangles blue, the squares red, and the hexagons green | 1. Color the cones blue, the cylinders red, and the spheres green. 2. Color the cylinders blue, the spheres red, and the cubes green
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw rotated flat shapes. Trace the dotted lines. Color the first shape GREEN and the second shape PURPLE.
  - **Brain Builder:** SORT & GROUP - D: In each group, draw an X on the shape that does not belong.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Puzzle-based fluency — Sorts rotated 2D and 3D shapes to teach that spatial orientation is a non-defining visual attribute.

##### 9. Identify Patterns working Forward with 2 Objects

- **Standards:** K.G.1
- **Prerequisites:** K-1
- **Description:** Use patterns to develop reasoning skills. Make the pattern A, B, A, B, A, B with objects. Identify the missing, last object. To introduce geometric shapes, make patterns with circles, triangles, squares, pentagons, and hexagons. How many sides does a triangle have? How many corners? How many of each does a square have?
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** What comes next? Circle the picture that continues each 2-object pattern
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw shapes to complete the alternating pattern.
  - **Brain Builder:** WHAT'S MY PATTERN? - A: In each row, fill the blank to complete the pattern.
  - **Tang strategy:** Patterns and skip counting; Reasoning and explanation — Extends alternating 2-object AB patterns forward to build sequential pattern recognition and reasoning.

##### 10. Identify Patterns working Forward with 3 Objects

- **Standards:** K.G.1
- **Prerequisites:** K-9
- **Description:** Continue to develop reasoning skills. Make the pattern A, B, C, A, B, C with objects. Identify the missing, last object. To introduce geometric solids, make patterns with spheres, cones, cylinders, cubes, and pyramids. How many flat sides does a cube have? How many corners? How many of each does a sphere or cone have?
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** What comes next? Circle the picture that continues each 3-object pattern
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw shapes to complete the alternating pattern.
  - **Brain Builder:** WHAT'S MY PATTERN? - B: In each row, draw the shape that completes the pattern.
  - **Tang strategy:** Patterns and skip counting; Reasoning and explanation — Extends 3-object ABC patterns using geometric solids to strengthen forward sequential reasoning.

##### 11. Identify Patterns working Backward

- **Standards:** K.G.1
- **Prerequisites:** K-10
- **Description:** Patterns are more challenging when the first object is missing, rather than the last object. When the "start" is unknown, start at the end and work backward. Read patterns from right-to-left, as well as from left-to-right. It's important for students to think flexibly from the earliest ages.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** What comes first? Circle the picture that comes first in each pattern
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw shapes to complete the repeating pattern.
  - **Brain Builder:** WHAT'S MY PATTERN? - C: In each row, draw the shape that completes the pattern.
  - **Tang strategy:** Patterns and skip counting; Reasoning and explanation — Determines missing initial objects in patterns by working backward from right to left to build flexible reasoning.

#### 2. Numbers to 5

**Question types in this unit:** Count and trace numerals (models); match quantities to digits; read picture graphs and write how many; order groups by counting up/down; cross off to reach zero; subitize "how many" and "how many more make 5".
**Tang lens:** Introduces the Concrete-to-Pictorial-to-Abstract (CPA) progression and subitizing to 5 using five-frames, snap cubes, tallies, and dice. It builds foundational part-total number sense that directly transitions into formal addition to 5 in Unit 3.

##### 12. Model and Trace Numbers to 5 using Five Frames

- **Standards:** K.CC.3
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Description:** Introduce numbers to describe how many objects in a group or set. Use 1-to-1 correspondence to count and quantify the number of counters on a five frame. Emphasize that the last number counted represents the whole group of objects, not the last object. Count and draw counters on five frames. Learn digits by tracing.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count and copy the model, then trace the digit four times | Count and copy the model, trace the digit twice, then write it twice
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many flat shapes? Color and count 1-3 shapes; trace the matching numbers.
  - **Brain Builder:** WRITING NUMBERS 1-5 - A: Use crayons to trace and then write the numbers in the colors shown.
  - **Tang strategy:** Concrete–pictorial–abstract; Number nicknames and place value — Uses five-frames and digit tracing to transition from concrete counters to abstract number symbols.

##### 13. Model and Trace Numbers to 5 using Snap Cubes

- **Standards:** K.CC.3
- **Prerequisites:** K-12
- **Description:** Use snap cubes as the go-to manipulative for modeling numbers concretely. They can be unsnapped or broken apart to find smaller parts. They can be snapped together or unitized to make the total or whole. Count, quantify, draw, and trace numbers to 5 using snap cubes.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count and copy the model, then trace the digit four times | Count and copy the model, trace the digit twice, then write it twice
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many cubes? Color and count 2-4 cubes; trace the matching numbers.
  - **Brain Builder:** WRITING NUMBERS 1-5 - B: Use crayons to trace and then write the numbers in the colors shown.
  - **Tang strategy:** Concrete–pictorial–abstract; Number nicknames and place value — Uses snap cubes as concrete manipulatives to unitize and model numbers to 5.

##### 14. Model and Trace Numbers to 5 using Tally Marks

- **Standards:** K.CC.3
- **Prerequisites:** K-13
- **Description:** Draw tally marks to model numbers pictorially. Use straws, stirrers, and toothpicks to model concretely, and continue tracing digits to represent numbers abstractly. Why is the 5th tally drawn horizontally or diagonally instead of vertically like the other 4? Want to unitize and show 1 group of 5, instead of 5 groups of 1.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count and copy the model, then trace the digit four times | Count and copy the model, trace the digit twice, then write it twice
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many tally marks? Trace and count 3-5 tally marks; trace the matching numbers.
  - **Brain Builder:** WRITING NUMBERS 1-5 - C: Use crayons to trace and then write the numbers in the colors shown.
  - **Tang strategy:** Concrete–pictorial–abstract; Number nicknames and place value — Uses tally marks to unitize groups of 5 and connect pictorial representations to numerals.

##### 15. Model and Trace Numbers to 5 using Dice Patterns

- **Standards:** K.CC.3
- **Prerequisites:** K-14
- **Description:** Use dice or dominoes to model numbers visually. Encourage students to recognize the different dot patterns so they can subitize – know instantly how many – without counting. Introduce part-total thinking as another way to quantify without counting. 3 is 2 and 1 more. 4 is 2 groups of 2. 5 is 4 plus 1 more in the middle.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count and copy the model, then trace the digit four times | Count and copy the model, trace the digit twice, then write it twice
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many small circles, or pips? Color and count 4-5 pips; trace the matching numbers.
  - **Brain Builder:** WRITING NUMBERS 1-5 - D: Use crayons to trace and then write the numbers in the colors shown.
  - **Tang strategy:** Concrete–pictorial–abstract; Mental math — Uses dice dot arrangements to encourage instant subitizing of quantities to 5 without counting.

##### 16. Model and Trace Numbers to 5 using Circular Arrangements

- **Standards:** K.CC.3
- **Prerequisites:** K-15
- **Description:** Use a circular arrangement of objects – a standard in some states – to model numbers visually. Start by counting to find how many, then transition to joining known, smaller parts to make the total, or whole. Working with circular arrangements when kids are young helps them later to read analog clocks and measure angles.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count and copy the model, then trace the digit four times | Count and copy the model, trace the digit twice, then write it twice
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many small circles? Color and count 4 small circles; trace the number.
  - **Brain Builder:** WRITING NUMBERS 1-5 - E: Use crayons to trace and then write the numbers in the colors shown.
  - **Tang strategy:** Concrete–pictorial–abstract; Mental math — Models numbers in circular patterns to build part-total spatial recognition and subitizing skills.

##### 17. Match Quantities to 5 with Objects & Digits

- **Standards:** K.CC.4
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-12 ○ K-1
- **Description:** Match the number of objects in each group to the correct digit. Figure out how many in each group by counting, joining known, smaller groups to make larger groups, and subitizing – knowing or recognizing how many without counting. See groups within groups – an important first step in developing part-whole thinking.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Draw lines to connect each quantity to the matching digit
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many round objects? Color and count basketballs; trace the number of basketballs.
  - **Brain Builder:** QUANTIFY to 5 - A: Color the shapes. Then trace the number that tells how many.
  - **Tang strategy:** Concrete–pictorial–abstract; Mental math — Matches object quantities to digits using subitizing and part-whole grouping.

##### 18. Match Quantities to 5 with Shapes & Digits

- **Standards:** K.CC.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-17 ○ K-3, K-2
- **Description:** Match the number of flat, 2D shapes or solid, 3D shapes in each group to the correct digit. Figure out how many in each group by counting, joining known, smaller groups to make larger groups, and subitizing – knowing how many without counting. Use geometric shapes to build familiarity with names and attributes.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Draw lines to connect each quantity to the matching digit
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many flat shapes? Color and count triangles; trace the number of triangles.
  - **Brain Builder:** QUANTIFY to 5 - B: Color the circles and squares. Then trace the number that tells how many.
  - **Tang strategy:** Concrete–pictorial–abstract; Mental math — Matches geometric 2D and 3D shape quantities to digits using instant visual recognition.

##### 19. Match Quantities to 5 with Coins & Digits

- **Standards:** K.CC.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-18 ○ K-4
- **Description:** Match the number of coins in each group to the correct digit. Figure out how many in each group by counting, joining known, smaller groups to make larger groups, and subitizing – knowing how many without counting. Use coins as counters – each with a value of 1 – to build familiarity with names and attributes.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Draw lines to connect each quantity to the matching digit
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many coins? Outline and count the nickels; trace the number of nickels.
  - **Brain Builder:** QUANTIFY to 5 - C: Trace the number that tells how many pennies and nickels.
  - **Tang strategy:** Concrete–pictorial–abstract; Mental math — Connects groups of coins to written digits by counting and subitizing sets of up to 5.

##### 20. Horizontal Picture Graphs to 5

- **Standards:** K.CC.5, K.CC.6
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Description:** Use picture graphs to represent numbers visually. Find how many in each row by counting. Build number sense by comparing. Is there a pattern? Does the next group or category have the same number of objects, or 1 more? Practice writing digits by tracing. When ready, practice writing digits from memory.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Read the horizontal picture graph; write how many of each item; answer the more/fewer questions
  - **Problem of the Day:** Count and compare the number of solid shapes.
  - **Brain Builder:** GRAPH IT - A: Color the animals. Then figure out how many and write the total at the top.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Concrete–pictorial–abstract — Compares horizontal picture graph categories to determine more or fewer items through visual alignment.

##### 21. Vertical Picture Graphs to 5

- **Standards:** K.CC.5, K.CC.6
- **Prerequisites:** K-20
- **Description:** Think flexibly by using vertical picture graphs to model and compare numbers. Find how many in each column by counting. Build number sense by comparing. Is there a pattern? Does the next group or category have the same number of objects, or 1 fewer? Practice writing digits, first by tracing and then from memory.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Read the vertical picture graph; write how many of each item; answer the more/fewer questions
  - **Problem of the Day:** Count and compare the number of solid shapes.
  - **Brain Builder:** GRAPH IT - B: Color the balls. Then figure out how many and write the total at the right side.
  - **Tang strategy:** Reasoning and explanation; Concrete–pictorial–abstract — Reads vertical picture graphs to compare quantities and answer more/fewer questions.

##### 22. Count Forward to 5

- **Standards:** K.CC.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-13
- **Description:** Make sense of numbers to 5 by putting sets of objects in increasing order. Transition from rote counting to understanding the natural numbers as a sequence of quantities that increases by 1. 2 is 1 more than 1. 3 is 1 more than 2. 4 is 1 more than 3, and 5 is 1 more than 4.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count up from 1; draw lines to connect the groups in counting order
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw arrows to count forward from 1 to 5.
  - **Brain Builder:** COUNT & CONNECT - A: Draw items. Then count forward from 1 and connect each number to 1 more.
  - **Tang strategy:** Patterns and skip counting; Reasoning and explanation — Orders quantities from 1 to 5 to understand number sequence as increasing by 1.

##### 23. Count Backward from 5

- **Standards:** K.CC.4
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-22
- **Description:** Count backward to deepen understanding of numbers 1 to 5 while laying the groundwork for subtraction. What number is 1 less and comes before 5? 4. What number is 1 less and comes before 4? 3. Put numbers represented visually in groups and abstractly with digits in descending or reverse order.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count down from 5; draw lines to connect the groups in counting order
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw arrows to count backward from 5 to 1.
  - **Brain Builder:** COUNT & CONNECT - B: Draw items. Then count backward from 5 and connect each number to 1 less.
  - **Tang strategy:** Patterns and skip counting; Mental math — Counts backward from 5 to 1 to build reverse sequence fluency and prepare for subtraction.

##### 24. Number Zero

- **Standards:** K.CC.3, K.CC.5
- **Prerequisites:** K-23
- **Description:** What does the quantity zero look like? Start with a set of objects and repeatedly take 1 object away. Start with 3. Take 1 away. Now there are 2. Take 1 away. Now there is 1. Take 1 away. How many are left? There are no objects left. That quantity is called "zero." Model with real objects, then cross off objects on five frames.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Cross off all the objects; write how many you crossed off and how many are left (zero)
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw arrows to count backward from 5 to 0.
  - **Brain Builder:** MAZE: Find your way from the top to the bottom. Draw a circle around the items that represent 0.
  - **Tang strategy:** Concrete–pictorial–abstract; Reasoning and explanation — Models zero concretely and pictorially by repeatedly taking away items until no objects remain.

##### 25. Subitize to 5 Part I

- **Standards:** K.CC.5
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-12
- **Description:** "Subitize" means to recognize how many quickly without counting. Show objects on five frames for a few seconds – not enough time to count. How many if the five frame is full? 5. How many if 1 is missing? 4. If 2 are missing? 3. Know how many quickly by subitizing how many are there or how many are missing.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Subitize each set; write how many | Write how many more make 5
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many flat shapes are missing?
  - **Brain Builder:** HOW MANY? - A: Write the correct number in the blank squares.
  - **Tang strategy:** Mental math; Make 10 / Make 20 / Make 100 — Subitizes five-frame visual models instantly to identify quantities and missing units that make 5.

##### 26. Subitize to 5 Part II

- **Standards:** K.CC.5
- **Prerequisites:** K-25
- **Description:** Encourage part-total (part-whole) thinking. Subitize both parts of a five frame – how many are there and how many are missing. Together, the 2 parts make 5 – the total. Subitize daily to build number sense, master combinations of 5, recognize shapes and solids, and learn geometric names.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Write how many; then write how many more make 5
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many solid shapes are missing? How many solid shapes do you see?
  - **Brain Builder:** HOW MANY? - B: Draw the correct number of circles and write how many and how many more make 5.
  - **Tang strategy:** Mental math; Number bonds — Subitizes both filled and empty parts of five-frames to master combinations that make 5.

#### 3. Add to 5

**Question types in this unit:** Count and write the total; trace parts/total on five frames; fill number bonds; complete addition equations; true/false equations; subitize five frames to find totals.
**Tang lens:** Develops the Part-Total Structural Framework and algebraic balance (Teeter Tots) by joining two known parts to find an unknown total using five-frames, number bonds, and addition equations. It extends Unit 2's number sense into operational fluency and sets up inverse subtraction in Unit 4.

##### 27. Quantify & Add to 5 using Five Frames

- **Standards:** K.CC.5
- **Prerequisites:** K-26
- **Description:** Use five frames to break a larger group – the total – into 2 smaller groups – the parts. Quantify how many are in each group, first by counting, and then by recognizing how many without counting. How many are in the first part? How many are in the second part? How many frames are empty? How many objects in all?
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count how many of each picture; then write how many in all (A + B)
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many flat shapes? Count and write how many in all.
  - **Brain Builder:** MATCHING - A: Draw lines from the five-frame on the left to the matching table on the right. Then write the missing total.
  - **Tang strategy:** Number bonds; Concrete–pictorial–abstract — Uses five-frames to decompose sets into two parts and combine them to find the total sum.

##### 28. Add Concretely by Identifying the Parts & Total

- **Standards:** K.OA.1
- **Prerequisites:** K-27
- **Description:** Use five frames to visually break a set of up to 5 counters into parts. Start by outlining each part. Count or subitize to know how many are in each group, and how many altogether. Recognize and read the words "part" and "total." Make sense of both by connecting the objects in the five frame with each digit being traced.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Trace the two parts and the total shown by each five-frame
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many solid shapes in all? Complete the parts and total model.
  - **Brain Builder:** MATCHING - B: Draw lines from the five-frame on the left to the matching table on the right. Then write the missing total.
  - **Tang strategy:** Number bonds; Concrete–pictorial–abstract — Identifies and traces explicit part-total relationships on five-frames to ground addition concepts.

##### 29. Add Concretely using Number Bonds

- **Standards:** K.OA.1
- **Prerequisites:** K-28
- **Description:** Introduce the number-bond symbol as a way to visualize the relationship between 2 parts and their total. Given 2 parts shown pictorially on a number-bond and concretely on a five frame, figure out the total – how many in all – by adding, grouping, subitizing, and counting, if needed.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Use the five-frame to find the total; write it in the number bond
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many in each part? How many in all?

#### Pedagogical Backbone

Greg Tang's pedagogical approach builds structural number sense, visual fluency, and mathematical reasoning through eight core principles aligned to Kindergarten content:

1. **Visual Subitizing Replaces Counting**: Instant visual recognition of quantities using five-frames, ten-frames, dice pips, and tally marks replaces unit-by-unit counting to accelerate the transition from perceptual counting to structural number sense.
2. **Part-Total Structural Framework**: Numbers and arithmetic operations are structured around part-whole relationships, treating addition as joining known parts to find an unknown total and subtraction as finding an unknown part from a total.
3. **Five and Ten Structural Anchoring**: Quantities from 6 to 9 are visualized as 5 and additional units, while teen numbers (11–19) are anchored at 10 and additional units to establish place-value foundations.
4. **Concrete-to-Pictorial-to-Abstract (CPA) Progression**: Conceptual understanding moves sequentially from physical manipulatives (snap cubes, coins) to visual representations (number bonds, picture graphs, ten-frames) and finally to abstract symbolic equations.
5. **Systematic Combinatorial Exploration**: Number decompositions and addend combinations are derived through ordered, systematic strategies like Shift 1 rather than randomized practice.
6. **Explicit Base-10 Language and Unitizing**: Multi-digit and teen numbers are spoken and written using Base-10 nicknames ("1 ten 7") to reinforce unitizing single units into tens before relying on standard number words.
7. **Algebraic Equivalence and Balance**: The equal sign is taught as a relational indicator of balance between two equivalent values through visual teeter-totters and true/false equations.
8. **Puzzle-Embedded Strategic Reasoning**: Computational fluency is developed through multi-constraint visual logic puzzles (Numtanga, Digicross, What's Wrong?) that require comparative thinking and error detection rather than rote drill sheets.

#### Overview

Kindergarten math centers on two main critical areas: representing, relating, and operating on whole numbers (starting with concrete sets of objects) and describing shapes and space. Students build foundational number sense by learning to count to 100 by ones and tens, recognize and write numbers from 0 to 20, and understand that each successive number name refers to a quantity that is one more. They count objects to tell "how many," compare numbers (using terms like greater than, less than, or equal to), and begin to model simple addition and subtraction as "putting together" or "taking apart" within 10. Place-value foundations emerge as students work with numbers 11-19, decomposing them into tens and ones. Measurement and data concepts introduce describing and comparing measurable attributes (length, weight, capacity) using direct comparison rather than tools. They classify objects into categories, count the number in each, and sort by attributes. Geometry focuses on identifying, naming, and describing basic two- and three-dimensional shapes (squares, circles, triangles, rectangles, cubes, cones, cylinders, spheres) in various orientations and sizes, while exploring spatial relationships like above, below, beside, and in front of. Throughout the year, students use concrete objects, drawings, and manipulatives to develop these ideas, laying the groundwork for more abstract thinking in later grades.
