#### 1. Tune Up

**Question types in this unit:** Quantify numbers on five and ten frames; subitize quick images; compare quantities in horizontal and vertical picture graphs; order sets and digits forward and backward; add and subtract fluently within 5; add and subtract within 10 using number bonds and ten frames; solve Add-To, Put-Together, Take-From, and Take-Apart word problems.

##### 1. Quantify Numbers to 5

- **Standards:** K.CC.5
- **Prerequisites:** K-12
- **Description:** Model numbers on a five frame. Count, add, or subitize – figure out quickly without counting – how many and how many are missing and needed to make 5 in all. Start with static images. Progress to quick images that limit processing time and build number sense. Encourage grouping instead of counting.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count objects on five frames; write the number of objects; write how many more make 5
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many frames are empty? How many flat shapes do you see? 1. 0 frames are empty, I see 5 circles. 2. 1 frame is empty, I see 4 triangles. 3. 2 frames are empty, I see 3 rectangles. 4. 3 frames are empty, I see 2 squares. 5. 4 frames are empty, I see 1 hexagon.
  - **Brain Builder:** HOW MANY? - A: Draw the correct number of circles. Then fill the blank squares with the missing numbers.

##### 2. Quantify Numbers to 10

- **Standards:** K.CC.5, K.OA.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-59 ○ K-26, K-25
- **Description:** Model numbers on a ten frame. How many counters? Use multiple strategies including counting all, counting on, adding the rows, and adding groups within a group. How many frames are empty? Knowing how many are missing makes figuring out how many are there, easier. Review and master all the ways to make 10.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Count objects on ten frames; write the number of objects; write how many more make 10
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many frames are empty? How many flat shapes do you see? 1. 2 frames are empty, I see 8 circles. 2. 4 frames are empty, I see 6 triangles. 3. 1 frame is empty, I see 9 rectangles. 4. 3 frames are empty, I see 7 squares.
  - **Brain Builder:** TEETER TOTS - A: Use numbers from the bank to correctly fill the blanks with the value (weight) of each shape.

##### 3. Subitize Numbers to 10

- **Standards:** K.CC.5, K.OA.4
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-68, K-67
- **Description:** Progress from static images, to finding combinations that make 10 on quick images. Show ten frames for 3 seconds or less to limit processing time, encourage grouping instead of counting, and build number sense. Use non-counting strategies to quickly figure out how many and how many more makes 10.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Subitize quick images of dots on ten frames; write the total number; write how many more make 10
  - **Problem of the Day:** Use the clues to figure out the number. Write the answer on the line and then color in the squares at the left to show the number.
  - **Brain Builder:** WHO AM I?: Use the clues to figure out the number. Write the answer on the line and then color in the squares at the left to show the number.

##### 4. Horizontal Picture Graphs to 10

- **Standards:** K.CC.5, K.CC.6
- **Prerequisites:** K-20
- **Description:** Use picture graphs to model and compare numbers. Add or count on from 5 to figure out how many. Read the graph's axis to check for accuracy. Use keywords to compare categories, including "more" and "fewer" for countable objects, and "greater" and "less" for numbers. Use shapes, solids, and coins for counters.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Read horizontal picture graphs; count objects in categories; compare amounts using more, fewer, greater, and less
  - **Problem of the Day:** Use the data in the graph to answer the questions below. 1. Are there more turtles or dogs? 2. How many more? 3. Are there fewer cats or turtles? 4. How many fewer? 5. How many animals in all?
  - **Brain Builder:** MORE OR LESS - A: Use the data in the graph to answer the questions below.

##### 5. Vertical Picture Graphs to 10

- **Standards:** K.CC.5, K.CC.6
- **Prerequisites:** 1-4 ○ K-21
- **Description:** Encourage flexibility by generalizing from horizontal to vertical picture graphs. Explore different ways to figure out how many. Emphasize grouping and adding strategies over counting strategies. Use shapes, solids, and U.S. coins for counters. Discuss their similarities, differences, and defining attributes.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Read vertical picture graphs; count objects in categories; compare amounts using more, fewer, greater, and less
  - **Problem of the Day:** Use the data in the graph to answer the questions below. 1. Are there fewer carrots or peaches? 2. How many fewer? 3. How many fruits and vegetables in all? 4. Are there more fruits or vegetables? 5. How many more?
  - **Brain Builder:** MORE OR LESS - B: Use the data in the graph to answer the questions below.

##### 6. Count Forward to 10

- **Standards:** K.CC.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-22
- **Description:** Put sets and digits in increasing order from 1 to 10. Use grouping strategies to figure out how many are in each set or group. When counting from 1 to 10, understand that each number represents a set with 1 more – not the last object counted. 8 is greater than 7. How much greater? 8 shapes are 1 more than 7 shapes.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Draw tally marks in boxes; count forward from 1 to 10; connect each number to 1 more
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw the correct number of tally marks in each box. Then count forward from 6. Draw lines to connect each number to 1 more.
  - **Brain Builder:** COUNT & CONNECT - A: Draw the correct number of tally marks in each box. Then count forward from 6. Draw lines to connect each number to 1 more.

##### 7. Add Fluently within 5

- **Standards:** K.OA.5
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-32 ○ K-31, K-30
- **Description:** Use equations and five frames to add within 5. What's 4+1? Visualize 4 on a five frame. Add 1 more. How many in all? The five frame is full, so total is 5. Check for accuracy by using or drawing counters. When ready, add mentally without five frames. Goal is fluency – adding quickly, accurately, and flexibly, without counting.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Add within 5 using five frames; write the addition equation and sum
  - **Problem of the Day:** Find your way from the top to the bottom of the maze. Circle the expression if the answer is 4.
  - **Brain Builder:** MAZE - A: Find your way from the top to the bottom of the maze. Circle the expression if the answer is 4.

##### 8. Add within 10 using Number Bonds & Ten Frames

- **Standards:** K.OA.1
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-78 ○ K-38
- **Description:** Use number bonds and ten frames to find the total. What's 5 and 3 more? Visualize 5 on a ten frame – the top row is full. Visualize 3 more below. How many in all? Since 2 are missing, the total is 8. Check for accuracy by drawing counters. Encourage students to check their thinking and make adjustments when needed.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Add within 10 using number bonds and ten frames; write the total sum
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw a line from each diagram on the left to the matching word problem on the right. Then fill in the missing totals.
  - **Brain Builder:** WORD PROBLEM MATCH - A: Draw a line from each diagram on the left to the matching word problem on the right. Then fill in the missing totals.

##### 9. Add-To Word Problems to 10

- **Standards:** K.OA.2
- **Prerequisites:** K-49 ○ 1-8
- **Description:** Solve "result unknown" problems. Given a starting amount ("start") and amount that's added to it ("change"), find the total ("result"). Start with learning problems that teach explicitly and don't require interpretation. Use a diagram to visualize the relationship between the parts and total. Transition to contextual story problems.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Solve Add-To result-unknown word problems to 10; fill in part-total models and write addition equations
  - **Problem of the Day:** Seth has 2 stickers. Jake gives Seth 4 more stickers. How many stickers does Seth have now?
  - **Brain Builder:** ADD-TO WORD PROBLEMS - A: Solve result-unknown word problems using visual part-total models.

##### 10. Put-Together Word Problems to 10

- **Standards:** K.OA.2
- **Prerequisites:** K-52, K-51 ○ 1-8
- **Description:** Given 2 parts, join them together to find the total. Start with explicit, learning problems that don't require interpretation. Use a visual model to show the relationship between the parts and total. If needed, use a ten frame to add the parts. When ready, transition to story problems that provide context.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Solve Put-Together word problems to 10; fill in part-total models and write addition equations
  - **Problem of the Day:** Tate drew 4 rectangles. Tate drew 2 squares. How many shapes did Tate draw in all?
  - **Brain Builder:** PUT-TOGETHER WORD PROBLEMS - A: Given 2 parts, join them together to find the total.

##### 11. Count Backward from 10

- **Standards:** K.CC.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-23 ○ K-65
- **Description:** Put sets and digits in decreasing order. Use grouping strategies to figure out how many are in each set. When counting down, understand that each number counted is 1 less than the previous number. 6 is 1 less than 7, but 6 shapes are 1 fewer than 7 shapes. Use "fewer" to compare countable objects.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Draw tally marks; count backward from 10 to 1; connect numbers to 1 less
  - **Problem of the Day:** Draw tally marks in each box. Then count backward from 9. Draw lines to connect each number to 1 less.
  - **Brain Builder:** COUNT & CONNECT - B: Draw the correct number of tally marks in each box. Then count backward from 9. Draw lines to connect each number to 1 less.

##### 12. Subtract Fluently within 5

- **Standards:** K.OA.5
- **Prerequisites:** K-47 ○ 1-7
- **Description:** Use equations and five frames to subtract within 5. What's 5-1? Visualize 5 – a full five frame. Take 1 away. How many are left? Since a five frame with 1 missing is 4, 5-1 = 4. Check for accuracy by using or drawing counters. When ready, subtract fluently without five frames – flexibly, accurately, and quickly.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Subtract fluently within 5 using five frames; write the subtraction equation and difference
  - **Problem of the Day:** Find your way through the maze from the top to the bottom. Circle the equation if it is TRUE.
  - **Brain Builder:** MAZE - B: Find your way from the top to the bottom of the maze. Circle the expression if the answer is 2.

##### 13. Subtract within 10

- **Standards:** K.OA.1
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-91 ○ K-95, K-48, K-43
- **Description:** Use number bonds and ten frames to subtract and find the unknown part. What's 9-3? Visualize 9 on a ten frame – a full top row and 4 more. Mentally cross off 3. There is 1 left. How many in all? 6, so 9-3 = 6. Check for accuracy by drawing counters. Encourage students to check their own thinking – metacognition.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Subtract within 10 using ten frames and number bonds; write the missing part and subtraction equation
  - **Problem of the Day:** Use the clues to figure out the number. Write the answer on the line.
  - **Brain Builder:** WHO AM I?: Use the clues to figure out the number. Write the answer on the line.

##### 14. Take-From Word Problems to 10

- **Standards:** K.OA.2
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-54 ○ 1-13, K-55
- **Description:** Use a visual model to solve a "take-from, result unknown" problem. Given the total (start) and how many are taken away (change), find how many are left (result). Use a visual model to show the relationship between the parts and total. Subtract or add to find the unknown part. Transition to story problems to provide context.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Solve Take-From result-unknown word problems to 10; fill in part-total models and write subtraction equations
  - **Problem of the Day:** Start with 6. Take some away. There is 1 left. How much was taken away?
  - **Brain Builder:** TAKE-FROM WORD PROBLEMS - A: Solve take-from result-unknown word problems using visual models.

##### 15. Take-Apart Word Problems to 10

- **Standards:** K.OA.2
- **Prerequisites:** K-57 ○ 1-14, K-58
- **Description:** Solve "take-apart" problems with both parts unknown. Given the total, how many could be in one part? How many would be in the other part? Since there are many ways to break a number apart, there is more than one answer. Start with explicit learning problems. Transition to contextual, story problems that need interpretation.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Solve Take-Apart word problems with both parts unknown to 10; find multiple part combinations for a given total
  - **Problem of the Day:** Find two different ways to break apart a total into parts.
  - **Brain Builder:** TAKE-APART WORD PROBLEMS - A: Find different ways to decompose a total into two parts.

#### 2. Operations to 10

**Question types in this unit:** Add and subtract within 10 with total, result, change, and start unknown; interpret horizontal and vertical picture graphs for addition and subtraction; determine the truth value of equations; balance addition and subtraction equations; compare numbers and expressions using greater than (>), less than (<), and equal (=) symbols.

##### 16. Add within 10 Total-Unknown

- **Standards:** 1.OA.8
- **Prerequisites:** K-79 ○ K-78, K-38
- **Description:** Use equations and ten frames to find the total. What's 6 and 3 more? Visualize 6 on a ten frame – the top row is full and 1 more. Visualize 3 more. How many in all? Since 1 is still missing, the total is 9. Check for accuracy by drawing counters. Encourage students to check their own thinking and make adjustments when needed.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Add within 10 with total unknown using ten frames and equations; write the sum
  - **Problem of the Day:** Bill picked 4 apples. Jill picked 3 apples. Write the letter 'B' on Bill's apples. Write the letter 'J' on Jill's apples. 1. How many apples did Bill and Jill pick in all?
  - **Brain Builder:** TEETER TOTS - B: Use numbers from the bank to correctly fill the blanks with the value (weight) of each shape.

##### 17. Add within 10 Picture Graphs

- **Standards:** 1.MD.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-63 ○ K-64, K-21, K-20, 1-16
- **Description:** Use horizontal and vertical picture graphs to provide context for addition. Add, compare, subitize, and read the graph's axis to figure out how many in each category. How many in 2 categories? Write an equation for the sum and solve by grouping in smart ways.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Read horizontal and vertical picture graphs; write addition equations for category sums within 10
  - **Problem of the Day:** Use the information in the graph to fill the blanks and answer the questions below. PJ picked 9 total apples. He picked 5 apples on Saturday. He picked some more apples on Sunday. How many apples did PJ pick on Sunday?
  - **Brain Builder:** GRAPH PROBLEMS - A: Use the information in the graph to fill the blanks and answer the questions below.

##### 18. Add within 10 Change-Unknown & Start-Unknown

- **Standards:** 1.OA.8
- **Prerequisites:** K-87, K-86 ○ K-80
- **Description:** Use ten frames to find an unknown part in an addition equation. 5 + __ = 7? Visualize 5 on a ten frame – a full top row. Now imagine 7 – a full top row and 2 more. What's the "change" – how many need to be added to 5 to make 7? Since 5+2 = 7, the part needing to be added is 2. Draw to check for accuracy.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Solve change-unknown and start-unknown addition equations within 10 using ten frames; write the missing addend
  - **Problem of the Day:** Start with a number. Add 1 more. The total is 7. What was the starting number?
  - **Brain Builder:** COMPARE AND SHIFT - A: Fill the blanks to make the expression on the right equal the expression on the left.

##### 19. Subtract within 10 Result-Unknown

- **Standards:** 1.OA.8
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 1-13 ○ K-96, K-95, K-94
- **Description:** Use equations and ten frames to subtract. What's 8 minus 3? Visualize a total of 8 on a ten frame – a full top row and 3 more. Imagine crossing off all 3. How many are left? There are still 5 left, so 8-3 = 5. Check for accuracy by drawing and crossing off counters. Make adjustments when needed.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Subtract within 10 with result unknown using ten frames and equations; write the difference
  - **Problem of the Day:** Gabe planted some trees on Monday. He planted 4 trees on Tuesday. How many trees did Gabe plant on Monday?
  - **Brain Builder:** TEETER TOTS - C: Fill the blanks with the correct symbols and numbers.

##### 20. Subtract within 10 Picture Graphs

- **Standards:** 1.MD.4
- **Prerequisites:** K-89 ○ 1-19, K-63
- **Description:** Use picture graphs to provide context for subtraction. Figure out how many in each category, then find the difference between any 2 categories. How many more or extra in the larger category? How many fewer or missing in the smaller category? Write an equation and find each difference.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Read horizontal and vertical picture graphs; write subtraction equations to compare categories within 10

##### 21. Subtract within 10 Change-Unknown & Start-Unknown

- **Standards:** 1.OA.8
- **Prerequisites:** 1-18 ○ K-92, K-91
- **Description:** Use ten frames to find an unknown part in a subtraction equation. 9 - __ = 6? Visualize a total of 9 on a ten frame – a full top row and 4 more. Now imagine 6. What's the "change" – how many need to be taken away from 9 to make 6? since 9-3 = 6, the part taken away is 3. Check for accuracy by drawing.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Solve change-unknown and start-unknown subtraction equations within 10 using ten frames; write the missing number
  - **Problem of the Day:** Start with 8. Take away some. There are 3 left. How many were taken away?

##### 22. Meaning of the Equal Sign

- **Standards:** 1.OA.7
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-45
- **Description:** Reinforce the meaning of "equal" by comparing the value of what is on the left side of the equal sign, to the value of what is on the right side. Is the equation "3 = 10-6" true or false? Since the value of 3 is 3, and the value of 10-6 is 4, the equation is false. The 2 sides of the equal sign do not have the same value.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Compare values on both sides of an equation; determine if the equation is true or false
  - **Problem of the Day:** Find your way through the maze from the top to the bottom. Circle the equation if it is TRUE.
  - **Brain Builder:** MAZE - C: Find your way through the maze from the top to the bottom. Circle the equation if it is TRUE.

#### 3. Teen Numbers

**Question types in this unit:** Subitize and quantify teen numbers using ten frames, linear models, picture graphs, and base 10 blocks; compare teen numbers and expressions; identify odd and even numbers; represent place value and expanded form to 20.

##### 31. Subitize Teen Numbers using Ten Frames

- **Standards:** 1.NBT.1
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** K-109 ○ K-103, K-68
- **Description:** Start by modeling teen numbers visually on static ten frames. Progress to quick images that encourage grouping and subitizing – figuring out how many quickly without counting – by limiting processing time. Subitize daily to develop number sense and lay the groundwork for place value.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Subitize teen numbers on double ten frames; write the total number and how many more make 20
  - **Problem of the Day:** How many more makes 2 tens, or 20? Find the missing part. 1. Outline counters to make 20. 2. Find the missing part. 3. Find the missing part.
  - **Brain Builder:** HOW MANY? - A: Draw the correct number of circles. Then fill the blank squares with the missing numbers.

##### 32. Compare Teen Numbers

- **Standards:** 1.NBT.3
- **Prerequisites:** K-108 ○ K-102
- **Description:** Use a "compare-the-parts" strategy to build number sense, encourage part-whole-thinking, and get ready to quantify the difference between 2 numbers. Is 13 greater than 11? Both have a full ten frame, so compare what's left. 3 is greater than 1, so 13 is greater than 11. Model numbers with ten frames, then number bonds.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Compare two teen numbers using double ten frames; write the greater than (>) or less than (<) symbol
  - **Problem of the Day:** In each row, fill in the dots in each ten-frame. Then put the correct < or > symbol between the numbers.
  - **Brain Builder:** DARE TO COMPARE - A: In each row, fill in the dots in each ten-frame. Then put the correct < or > symbol between the numbers.

##### 33. Even vs Odd*

- **Standards:** 1.OA.5, 2.OA.3
- **Description:** Enrich by comparing a set of numbers and determine if the largest and smallest numbers are even or odd. To determine if a number is even or odd, model it with a row of evenly spaced dots. If every dot can be paired with a partner, the number is even. #### 7. Fraction Foundations

**Question types in this unit:** Identify equal lengths and equal parts of bars; name halves, thirds, and fourths; model fractional parts with bars, squares, and circles; represent unit fractions; compare and match unit fractions.

##### 77. Identify Equal Lengths & Equal Parts of Bars

- **Standards:** 1.MD.1
- **Prerequisites:** K-131 ○ K-71
- **Description:** Lay the foundation for fractions by identifying bars with equal lengths. Start by stacking and aligning 3 bars to make comparing easier. Which 2 have the same length? Which bar is the longest? Which is the shortest? Transition to bars that are side-by-side – parts of a whole bar – and not as easy to compare, visually.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Identify equal lengths and equal parts of bars; determine which bar is divided into equal parts and count parts
  - **Problem of the Day:** Max is cutting firewood. He cuts a tree trunk into 4 logs that are all the same length. Which of the logs below did Max cut? Explain your thinking.
  - **Brain Builder:** RAISE THE BAR! - A: Use the clues to correctly color the bars below.

##### 78. Name Halves, Thirds & Fourths

- **Standards:** 1.G.3, 1.MD.2
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 1-77 ○ K-132
- **Description:** Divide a bar – the whole – into 2, 3, or 4 equal parts. Learn their names – 1 half, 1 third, and 1 fourth. How many make the whole? 2 halves, 3 thirds, and 4 fourths. Model the part-whole relationship concretely with real and virtual bars, and pictorially with pictures of bars and by drawing bars.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Name equal parts of a whole bar as halves, thirds, and fourths; write number of parts and name of each part
  - **Problem of the Day:** Divide a bar into equal parts and write the name of each part: half, third, or fourth.
  - **Brain Builder:** RAISE THE BAR! - B: Use the clues to correctly color the bars below. Then draw lines to connect each fraction name with the matching bar.

##### 79. Model Halves, Thirds & Fourths with Bars

- **Standards:** 1.G.3
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 1-78 ○ K-133
- **Description:** Start with 1-dimensional, linear models. Divide the length of a bar into 2, 3, or 4 equal parts. To draw 2 halves or 3 thirds, estimate visually. To draw 4 fourths, first draw 2 halves. Divide each half in half to make 4 equal parts.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Model halves, thirds, and fourths by partitioning bars into equal parts; color designated fraction parts
  - **Problem of the Day:** Follow the prompts to correctly divide and color the bars. Divide the longest bar into fourths, shortest into halves, other into thirds.
  - **Brain Builder:** RAISE THE BAR! - C: Follow the prompts to correctly divide and color the bars.

##### 80. Model Halves, Thirds & Fourths with Squares

- **Standards:** 1.G.3
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 1-79 ○ K-134
- **Description:** Generalize from dividing the length of a bar to dividing the area or space "inside" a square into equal parts. Explore different ways of drawing 2 halves, 3 thirds, and 4 fourths – using horizontal, vertical, and diagonal lines. Fold paper to model and reinforce equal parts concretely.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Model halves, thirds, and fourths with squares; partition squares into equal area parts and color unit fractions
  - **Problem of the Day:** Partition squares into 2, 3, or 4 equal parts and name the fraction parts.
  - **Brain Builder:** FRACTION SQUARES - A: Divide squares into equal parts and color designated fraction parts.

##### 81. Model Halves, Thirds & Fourths with Circles

- **Standards:** 1.G.3
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 1-80
- **Description:** Divide circles into equal parts by first drawing a dot in the center. Draw a line thru the dot to draw 2 halves. Draw 3 lines from the edge of the circle to the center to draw 3 thirds. To draw 4 fourths, draw 2 halves and divide each half in half. To model fractional parts of a circle concretely, use a cookie, pizza, or paper plate.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Model halves, thirds, and fourths with circles; partition circles from center point into equal parts and color unit fractions
  - **Problem of the Day:** Partition circles from center point into equal parts and color unit fractions.
  - **Brain Builder:** FRACTION CIRCLES - A: Divide circles into equal parts from center dot and color unit fractions.

##### 82. Model Unit Fractions with Bars

- **Standards:** 1.G.3
- **Prerequisites:** 1-79
- **Description:** Partition the length of a bar into 2, 3, or 4 equal parts. See and understand halves, thirds, and fourths as equal, countable parts in a whole. Color 1 part. 1 out of 2 equal parts is 1 half of the whole. 1 out of 3 equal parts is 1 third. 1 out of 4 equal parts is 1 fourth or 1 quarter. Note that any of the equal parts can be colored.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Model unit fractions with bars; color 1 part out of equal parts and name the unit fraction (1 half, 1 third, 1 fourth)
  - **Problem of the Day:** Identify and color 1 out of equal parts of a bar to represent a unit fraction.
  - **Brain Builder:** UNIT FRACTIONS BARS - A: Color 1 part of the bar and name the unit fraction.

##### 83. Model Unit Fractions with Squares

- **Standards:** 1.G.3
- **Prerequisites:** 1-82 ○ 1-80
- **Description:** Generalize from the length of a bar to the space or area inside a square. Draw and divide a square into 2, 3, or 4 equal parts. Reinforce understanding of unit fractions by coloring any 1 of the parts to show 1 half, 1 third, or 1 fourth. When the square or whole is the same, 1 half > 1 third, 1 half > 1 fourth, and 1 third > 1 fourth.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Model unit fractions with squares; partition square into equal parts and color 1 part to represent unit fraction
  - **Problem of the Day:** Color 1 equal part of a square to represent a unit fraction.
  - **Brain Builder:** UNIT FRACTIONS SQUARES - A: Partition square and color 1 part to show unit fraction.

##### 84. Model Unit Fractions with Circles

- **Standards:** 1.G.3
- **Prerequisites:** 1-83 ○ 1-81
- **Description:** Generalize from squares to circles. Reinforce understanding of unit fractions by identifying and drawing 1 half, 1 third, and 1 fourth. Start by drawing a circle with a dot in the center. Partition the circle into 2, 3, or 4 equal parts. Draw fourths by dividing halves in half. Color 1 of the equal parts to represent a unit fraction.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Model unit fractions with circles; partition circle and color 1 part to represent unit fraction
  - **Problem of the Day:** Color 1 equal part of a circle to represent a unit fraction.
  - **Brain Builder:** UNIT FRACTIONS CIRCLES - A: Partition circle and color 1 part to show unit fraction.

##### 85. Compare Unit Fractions

- **Standards:** 1.MD.2
- **Prerequisites:** 1-82
- **Description:** Compare the unit fractions 1 half, 1 third, and 1 fourth. When the whole is the same, more equal parts mean smaller parts. Fewer equal parts mean larger parts. There is an inverse relationship between the number of pieces and size of equal pieces. Length models – colored rods and bars – make comparing fractions, easier.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Compare unit fractions (1 half vs 1 third vs 1 fourth); determine which unit fraction is larger or smaller
  - **Problem of the Day:** Compare unit fractions of the same whole. Determine which fraction part is larger or smaller.
  - **Brain Builder:** COMPARE FRACTIONS - A: Compare unit fractions and write >, <, or =.

##### 86. Match Unit Fractions

- **Standards:** 1.G.3
- **Prerequisites:** 1-85 ○ K-134
- **Description:** Use conventional and unconventional representations of unit fractions to enrich, assess, and deepen understanding. Vary the part that's shaded, vary the whole to include equilateral triangles and rotated squares, and lay the foundation for fraction notation by writing "1 half," "1 third," and "1 fourth" – vertically.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Match visual fraction representations to unit fraction names (1 half, 1 third, 1 fourth)
  - **Problem of the Day:** Match pictures of partitioned shapes to fraction names.
  - **Brain Builder:** FRACTION MATCH - A: Draw lines connecting visual fraction models to their matching fraction names.

#### 10. Operations to 100

**Question types in this unit:** Add single-digit numbers with sums to 60 and 100 using Make 20 and Make 10s strategies with base 10 blocks and number bonds; add multiples of 10 and solve word problems with multiples of 10; add two-digit numbers with regrouping.

##### 108. Add a Single-Digit Number with No Regrouping

- **Standards:** 1.NBT.4
- **Prerequisites:** 1-49
- **Description:** Add 2-digit and 1-digit numbers with no regrouping. First add the ones, then add back the tens to get the total. What's 26+2? Since 6+2 = 8 and 20+8 = 28, 26+2 = 28. Start by modeling numbers with concrete Base 10 Blocks or snap cubes. Progress to using pictorial number bonds and finally, just mental math.
- **Question types:**

##### 109. Add using the Make 20 Strategy

- **Standards:** 1.NBT.4
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 1-45
- **Description:** Generalize the Make 10 strategy to the Make 20 strategy. Continue using ten frames to model the parts and total. What's 18 + 6? Break 6 into 2 parts. First make 20, then add the rest. Since 6 = 2 + 4, add 18 + 2 = 20, then add 20 + 4 = 24. Encourage students to think in groups of 10 whenever possible.
- **Question types:**

##### 110. Add using the Make 10s Strategy with Base 10 Blocks

- **Standards:** 1.NBT.4
- **Prerequisites:** 1-45
- **Description:** Generalize from making 20 to making the next multiple of 10. Use Base 10 blocks or snap cubes instead of ten frames. What is 25 + 8? Break 8 into 2 parts. First make 30, then add the rest. 8 = 5 + 3, so add 25 + 5 = 30, then add 30 + 3 = 33. Think in groups of 10.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Add using Make 10s strategy with base 10 blocks (sums to 60); regroup 10 ones as 1 ten rod
  - **Problem of the Day:** Use Make 10s strategy with base 10 blocks to add a single-digit number to a 2-digit number.

##### 111. Add using the Make 10s Strategy with Number Bonds

- **Standards:** 1.NBT.4
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 1-110
- **Description:** Transition from concrete Base 10 blocks to pictorial number bonds. What's 56+6? Think of 6 as 4 and 2. Since 56+4 = 60 and 60+2 = 62, 56+6 = 62. When ready, progress from drawing a number bond, to mentally visualizing a number in parts. What's 48+7? Visualize 7 as 2 and 5. Add to 2 to make 50, then add 5 to get 55.
- **Question types:**

  - **Problem of the Day:** Add using Make 10s strategy with number bonds for sums up to 100.

##### 114. Add 2-Digit + Multiple of 10: Base 10 Blocks & Discs

##### 115. Add 2-Digit + Multiple of 10: Number Bonds & Mental Math

#### 11. Measurement

**Question types in this unit:** Measure and compare 2 and 3 lengths using nonstandard units; measure from any starting point; analyze measurement with gaps and overlaps; explore inverse relationship of unit size; add equal groups and 3 numbers in geometric contexts; identify, compare, and add U.S. coins; tell and write time in whole and half hours for morning, noon, and night.

##### 121. Measure and Compare 2 Lengths

- **Standards:** 1.MD.2
- **Prerequisites:** K-70 ○ K-69
- **Description:** Measure and compare the lengths of colored bars in informal units – large and small paper clips. Explore multiple strategies to find the difference – how much longer or shorter one bar is than the other. Count the extra or missing paper clips, add on to the shorter bar, and subtract the shorter bar from the longer bar.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Measure and compare 2 lengths using paper clips; write length of bar A, length of bar B, and how much longer/shorter
  - **Problem of the Day:** Compare to find the length of each bar, measured in paper clips. 1. Bar A is 5 paper clips longer than Bar B. 2. Bar B is 3 paper clips shorter than Bar C.
  - **Brain Builder:** MEASURE UP! - A: Use the clues to figure out the color of each pencil.

##### 122. Measure and Compare 3 Lengths

- **Standards:** 1.MD.1, 1.MD.2
- **Prerequisites:** 1-121
- **Description:** Generalize from comparing 2 bars to comparing 3 bars. Which bar is the shortest? Which is the longest? Put the bars in order from shortest to longest. How much longer or shorter is one bar than another? Count, add, or subtract to quantify their differences. Use different strategies to check for accuracy.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Measure and compare 3 lengths (bars A, B, C) using paper clips; write equations comparing bar lengths
  - **Problem of the Day:** Compare bars and find their difference. Show your thinking with equations.
  - **Brain Builder:** COMPARE 3 LENGTHS - A: Compare 3 bar lengths measured in paper clips and write difference equations.

##### 123. Measure and Compare Lengths from Any Starting Point

- **Standards:** 1.MD.1, 1.MD.2
- **Notes:** Co-presented
- **Prerequisites:** 1-121
- **Description:** To understand length as the shortest distance between 2 points, measure bars that are not aligned with the first unit. This is analogous to measuring objects with a ruler that are not aligned with zero. Measure from right-to-left, and use known lengths to figure out unknown lengths by adding or subtracting units.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Measure and compare lengths from any starting point (unaligned bars); subtract starting position from ending position
  - **Problem of the Day:** Measure bars that are not aligned with zero. Subtract start position from end position to find length.
  - **Brain Builder:** MEASURE UNALIGNED - A: Measure bars from non-zero starting points and calculate lengths.

##### 124. Analyze Measurement with Gaps & Overlaps

- **Standards:** 1.MD.2
- **Prerequisites:** 1-123
- **Description:** Analyze measuring mistakes to deepen understanding about length. If the units have gaps between them, is the true length longer or shorter than the observed number of units? Longer. What if the units overlap? The true length is shorter. To measure correctly, the units must be aligned end-to-end with no gaps or overlaps.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Analyze measurement with gaps and overlaps; identify why measurement is invalid when units have gaps or overlaps
  - **Problem of the Day:** Analyze measurement validity. Determine if paper clip placement has gaps or overlaps.
  - **Brain Builder:** MEASUREMENT ANALYSIS - A: Identify correct measurement technique without gaps or overlaps.

##### 125. Big Units vs Small Units - Inverse Relationship

- **Standards:** 1.MD.2
- **Prerequisites:** 1-124 ○ 1-85
- **Description:** Explore the relationship between the size and number of units by measuring objects twice. Measure first with large paper clips, then measure with small paper clips. Which requires more paper clips? Need more, smaller paper clips, and fewer, large paper clips. There's an inverse relationship between the size and number of units.
- **Question types:**
  - **Workbook:** Explore inverse relationship between unit size and number of units; compare measuring with large vs small paper clips
  - **Problem of the Day:** Compare measuring an object with small vs large paper clips. Understand that larger units yield smaller counts.
  - **Brain Builder:** UNIT SIZE RELATIONSHIP - A: Compare measuring results using different sized units.

##### 126. Geometric Measurement - Add Equal Groups

- **Standards:** 1.MD.2
- **Prerequisites:** K-73 ○ K-72
- **Description:** Use shapes to provide context for counting, skip counting, and adding, and also build a foundation for both multiplication and measurement. If each side of an equilateral triangle has length 4, what's the total length of all 3 sides? What's 4+4+4? Since 4+4 = 8 and 8+4 = 12, the total length of all 3 sides – 3 groups of 4 – is 12.
- **Question types:**

  - **Problem of the Day:** Add equal side lengths of regular 2D shapes to calculate total perimeter.
  - **Brain Builder:** GEOMETRIC PERIMETER EQUAL GROUPS - A: Write repeated addition equations for perimeter of shapes with equal sides.

##### 127. Geometric Measurement - Add 3 Numbers

- **Description:** Use geometric shapes to provide context for finding the sum of 3 addends. Given 3 shapes, write an equation for the total number of sides. Use a Make 10 strategy to add in smart ways. How many sides all together in a hexagon, pentagon, and rectangle? What's 6+5+4? Since 6+4 = 10 and 10+5 = 15, there are 15 sides in all.

##### 130. Tell Time - Learn the Hour Hand

- **Standards:** 1.MD.3
- **Description:** Learn to read time on an analog clock. Learn to write time using digital notation. Start with a clock that has only the shorter, hour hand. ##### 131. Tell Time - Learn the Minute Hand

- **Description:** Read a clock with just the longer, minute hand. The digits 1 to 12 still represent hours, so connect each hour with minutes. 12 is 0 min, 3 is 15 min, 6 is 30 min, and 9 is 45 min. If the minute hand points at 12, it's 0 min after the hour. If it points at 6, it's 30 min after the hour – 1 half hour. If it points at 2, it's 10 min after the hour.

##### 132. Tell Time - Whole Hours & Half-Hours

- **Description:** Put hours and minutes together by reading a clock that has both hands. What time is it if the hour hand points at 2 and the minute hand points at 12? It's 2:00 – 2 hours and 0 minutes after 12:00. What time is it if the hour hand points at 1 and the minute hand points at 6? It's 1:30 – 1 hour and 30 minutes after 12:00, – or "half past 1."
- **Question types:**

  - **Problem of the Day:** Match analog clock faces showing whole hours and half-hours to digital times.
  - **Brain Builder:** TELL TIME MATCHING - A: Connect analog clock faces to digital times in whole and half hours.

- **Description:** Use context to make sense of clock times by matching events to times in the morning, afternoon, and night. #### Overview

First-grade math builds directly on Kindergarten foundations, with focus on four critical areas: developing understanding of addition and subtraction (and related strategies) within 20; exploring whole-number relationships and place value (especially grouping in tens and ones); understanding linear measurement as iterating length units; and reasoning about attributes of, and composing/decomposing, geometric shapes. Students represent and solve word problems involving addition and subtraction, learn properties like the commutative and associative laws, and become fluent with facts within 20. They extend the counting sequence, understand that two-digit numbers are composed of tens and ones, and use place-value strategies to add or subtract within 100. In measurement and data, students measure lengths with nonstandard and standard units, compare lengths, tell and write time in hours and half-hours, and work with money (identifying coins and their values). They organize, represent, and interpret data with simple graphs. Geometry deepens as students distinguish defining attributes (e.g., number of sides or corners) from non-defining ones, compose two-dimensional shapes to create new ones, and partition shapes into equal shares while describing the parts. Hands-on activities and visual models help students connect these concepts, fostering both procedural fluency and conceptual understanding.
